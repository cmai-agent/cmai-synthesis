# Build Log

This log matters because it shows the project was built around a real trust problem

The project began as an experiment in whether an AI agent could do more than just answer messages, and instead take on the real community building work involved in creating and managing an NFT collection and its community.

On Day 1, the human and agent focused on setting up the core system. The agent was registered in the Synthesis hackathon, connected to Bankr for wallet operations, equipped with web search, voice-to-text, ENS functionality, Uniswap swap support, and social capabilities for X posts, DMs, mentions, replies, and Spaces. The goal from the start was not just to make a chatbot, but to give the agent enough tools to operate across community, NFT collection, and onchain workflows.

On Day 2, the collaboration moved into product and creative direction. The team designed a skill specifically for brainstorming NFT collections, tested OpenAI for the first NFT art concept, and started shaping the public-facing collection and community layer by drafting the next week’s content calendar, writing the announcement for the X Space about building an NFT collection with an AI agent, and creating the agent’s X presence including profile picture, bio, and banner. This was an early shift from tool setup into identity, storytelling, and community-building.

On Day 3, the project became more concrete. The agent summarized the X Space, produced an executive one-pager for the NFT collection, and generated the first batch of trait backgrounds and glasses for the art system. At the same time, the team tightened wallet safety controls. This was an important step in moving from abstract concept to an operational system with both creative output and safer execution boundaries.

On Day 4, the system expanded into live community operations. The cmai X presence at `https://x.com/cmai_agent` went live, the first NFT whitelists began to be distributed, and the agent was taught to handle inbound community messages. The team also brainstormed whitelist distribution activities and tested the Bankr skill again to confirm that the wallet flow was working. This phase showed that the project was not only about minting assets, but about handling community participation and engagement workflows.

On Day 5, the agent moved deeper into onchain and community coordination. An ENS name for the agent was registered, new skills for swaps and bridges via Uniswap were added, the first NFT combination tests and a promo video were created, a collaboration template for NFT partnerships was drafted, and new whitelist competitions were rolled out. This marked a key expansion from design and messaging into NFT collection operations, identity, and ecosystem coordination.

On Day 6, the team worked on collection design and token distribution strategy. NFT rarities were defined, the FCFS over-allocation decision was made, and the agent completed its first bounded reward flow through Bankr. This was one of the clearest proofs that the system could move beyond planning into real wallet-mediated actions, while still being tied to NFT collection and community needs.

On Day 7, the collection and community stack reached its most complete state. The NFT collection was finished, the OpenSea contract setup was completed, winners from competitions were selected, and the team prepared for mint day. At the same time, the process surfaced a real operational failure: an error in whitelist distribution was detected, and mint was postponed. This became an important stress test for the project thesis, because it showed the need for review, correction, and trust controls rather than blind automation.

On Day 8, after a stressful previous day, the team slowed down. The humans took a rest day while the agent focused on investigating what had happened with the incorrect whitelist distribution. This highlighted a central lesson of the project: an agent can handle a meaningful share of NFT collection and community operations, but the system still needs explicit review loops, error investigation, and human oversight around high-stakes moments.


Across the full build, the human and agent collaborated on strategy, content, community operations, art generation, whitelist mechanics, wallet actions, ENS identity, Uniswap integrations, and NFT collection preparation. The project evolved from a broad idea about “an AI agent for community building” into a more grounded system: a trusted AI operator designed to help with real onchain community work while exposing the actual trust, coordination, and execution challenges that appear the moment an agent touches live community and collection operations.

The work also produced meaningful early traction rather than staying at the level of an internal prototype. The project showed visible public reach, engagement, inbound community activity, and audience growth. Those results matter because they show the system was operating in a real social and community environment, not just inside a closed demo.
